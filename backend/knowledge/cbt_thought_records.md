# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) Thought Records & Cognitive Reframing

## Core Concept
Thoughts are mental events, not objective facts. How we interpret an event directly influences our physiological and emotional response.

## Common Cognitive Distortions
1. **Catastrophizing:** Expecting the worst possible outcome (e.g., "If I fail this one test, my entire degree and career are ruined").
2. **All-or-Nothing (Black-and-White) Thinking:** Viewing situations in extremes with no middle ground (e.g., "If it's not perfect, I am a total failure").
3. **Mind Reading:** Assuming you know what others negatively think about you without concrete evidence (e.g., "They haven't replied to my text, they must hate me").
4. **Emotional Reasoning:** Believing that because you feel anxious, danger must be imminent (e.g., "I feel overwhelmed, so I won't survive this interview").
5. **Should Statements:** Imposing rigid moral or performance demands on yourself (e.g., "I should never feel tired; I should be studying 14 hours every single day").

## Simple 4-Step Thought Record Protocol
1. **Identify the Triggering Situation:** What happened? Who was there? When did the distress begin?
2. **Unpack the Automatic Thought:** What words or prediction ran through your mind in that exact moment?
3. **Examine the Evidence:**
   - What real, factual evidence supports this thought?
   - What factual evidence contradicts or weakens this thought?
   - Has a similar fear happened before, and how did you actually cope?
4. **Formulate a Balanced Alternative:** Create a nuanced, grounded statement: "While this exam is challenging, I have prepared parts of it, and one test does not determine my intrinsic worth or future."
